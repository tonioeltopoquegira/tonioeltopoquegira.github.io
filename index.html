Research

My research focuses on methods to solve design, optimization or search (here used almost interchangeably, in the computational sense) problems in science and engineering. These problems are typically dependent on the solution of ordinary or partial differential equation, for instance the Boltzmann Transport Equation for nanoscale thermal devices, or the constitutive equations for mechanical systems. Many complexities arise: high-dimensionality of the design space, expensiveness of the forward solvers, transferability of the design knowledge to new objectives. Other challenges are the multi-objective nature of design or the necessity to optimize under uncertainty for robust designs. I try to develop methods to enable, accelerate or improve these tasks using Artificial Intelligence, particularly Deep Learning.

Background

I was born and raised in Padova, Italy. After high school I moved to Milan, where in 2023 I graduated with a B.Sc. in Economics and Computer Science from Bocconi University.

I then moved to Georgia Tech to pursue master's degrees in Computational Science and one in Mathematics. During my master's I started collaborating with Prof. Raphaël Pestourie. I also had two summer research experiences: at the Technical University of Munich (TUM), in the group of Prof. Wolfgang Polifke, working on scientific machine learning for thermoacoustic instabilities in combustion engines; and at Los Alamos National Laboratory, under the supervision of Dr. Nicholas Lubbers and Dr. Yulia Pimonova, working on ML for molecular search.

During my master's I helped my roommates found Deleon Technologies, a startup that aims to miniaturize and democratize chemical analysis through hardware and software. In mid 2025, the startup has raised around $1M at an $8M valuation.

From Fall 2025 I transferred to the Ph.D. program in Computational Science and Engineering at Georgia Tech, where I currently focus on scientific machine learning, more specifically on AI-enhanced PDE-constrained optimization. I frequently collaborate with Los Alamos National Lab and with the group of Prof. Steven G. Johnson at MIT.

Marine life

I have been passionate about the marine world since I was very young. For the Sustainable Design course, together with the talented Yangyang He, I had fun designing, building and validating POLYPOD, a prototype of an artificial coral reef for coral restoration and coastal defence.

POLYPOD is a modular ceramic reef unit, slip-cast from a single reusable plaster mold, that aims to become the substrate for future coral gardens. CFD simulation shows it dissipating about 60% of incoming wave energy. POLYPOD was a spotlight at the School of Industrial Design 2026 Showcase. Click here for the full pictorial (PDF).
