Projects

My research focuses on methods to solve design, optimization or search (here used almost interchangeably, in the computational sense) problems in science and engineering. These problems are typically dependent on the solution of ordinary or partial differential equation, for instance the Boltzmann Transport Equation for nanoscale thermal devices, or the constitutive equations for mechanical systems. Many complexities arise: high-dimensionality of the design space, expensiveness of the forward solvers, transferability of the design knowledge to new objectives. Other challeges are the multi-objective nature of design or the necessity to optimize under uncertainty for robust designs. I try to develop methods to enable, accelerate or improve these tasks using Artificial Intelligence, particularly Deep Learning.

Transferable inverse design of mechanical metamaterials

Within five percentage points of the best gradient-based result using 43% of its computational budget, and five to seventeen times fewer evaluations than CMA-ES.

The problem

Mechanical metamaterials get their behavior from internal structure, so choosing the structure is a design problem. The properties that matter in practice, such as how much energy a structure absorbs when it is crushed, are nonlinear. The design space is high-dimensional and every candidate needs an expensive, often non-differentiable simulation, so gradient-free optimizers need thousands of evaluations.

The approach

TRAMA (TRAnsferable representation learning for Metamaterial Architectures) builds the search space from cheap linear physics. Early-stopped, gradient-based optimization of the linear Young's modulus yields a small library of structures. Physics-informed augmentation expands it more than 200-fold, and a pre-trained vision-transformer autoencoder compresses it into an eight-dimensional latent space. Bayesian optimization then searches that space for nonlinear properties.

What we found

  • Energy-absorption efficiency within five percentage points of the best gradient-based result, using 43% of its computational budget.
  • Five to seventeen times fewer evaluations than CMA-ES.
  • Fabricated and tested designs reproduce the simulated stress–strain response to within 5.3%.
  • A post-hoc analysis maps what transfers to new properties and where the pipeline reaches its limits.

Where it applies

Any structure designed for nonlinear mechanical response, with energy absorption as the main example, including biomedical and soft robotic devices. The same idea applies wherever cheap physics can pretrain a representation used for expensive physics.

Related publication: Efficient Optimization of Mechanical Metamaterials via Transferable Representation Learning

Meta-learning for multi-objective molecular discovery

On a real large-scale search for spin-crossover metal–organic complexes, a baseline performs 78% worse in Pareto sense than the meta-learned surrogate.

The problem

Useful molecules have to balance several properties at once, so the goal is a Pareto front of trade-offs rather than a single best candidate. The space of synthetically accessible molecules is enormous, and each property comes from costly quantum-level chemistry. Deep learning models struggle with that budget and rarely explain themselves.

The approach

The pipeline puts interpretable linear meta-learning models into an Efficient Global Optimization framework. The surrogates are trained across several chemical objectives and cheap auxiliary properties, so a new objective starts from what earlier ones already taught them, and their weights tell a chemist which features matter. A Bayesian bootstrap of linear models gives a distribution for each prediction.

Good candidates sit in the tails of that distribution, where standard uncertainty estimates fail. An adaptive confidence-tuning step recalibrates the exploration–exploitation trade-off as the search evolves.

What we found

  • On a real large-scale search for spin-crossover metal–organic complexes, the baseline performs 78% worse in Pareto sense than the meta-learning alternative.
  • Dynamic confidence tuning dominates over 50% of the statically calibrated front.
  • Fronts are compared with the C-metric for domination and star discrepancy for how evenly they cover objective space, so a smaller front is not mistaken for a better one.
  • The pipeline is massively parallel and runs on Los Alamos HPC with 600 CPUs.

Where it applies

Any discovery loop with several competing objectives and expensive evaluations, where scientists need to trust and understand the model that is steering the search.

Related publication: Interpretable Meta-learning for Multi-objective Chemical Search

In review. With A. Hammond, G. Romano, R. Pestourie and S. G. Johnson.

Adaptive convex approximations for constrained stochastic optimization

Convergence guarantees, and up to two orders of magnitude faster than classical constrained stochastic methods on standard problems.

The problem

Many machine learning and engineering problems have constraints, and their objectives and constraints can only be estimated from noisy samples. Classical constrained methods assume exact values, and stochastic methods usually handle constraints poorly or slowly.

The approach

At each iteration the algorithm builds a convex approximation of the noisy problem and solves it, then adapts the approximation as it learns about the problem. The convergence analysis builds on the theory of stochastic successive convex approximation, extended to cover the adaptive updates and the averaging of noisy estimates.

What we found

  • On a set of classic problems the method converges up to two orders of magnitude faster than classical constrained stochastic optimization.
  • It lowers the worst-label classification error on MNIST, a case where one class matters more than the average.
  • It lets you choose a specific trade-off between KL divergence and reconstruction error while training a variational autoencoder.

Where it applies

Fairness and worst-case constraints in learning, trade-off control in generative models, and engineering design where constraints are measured with noise.

Related publication: Adaptive Convex Approximations for Constrained Stochastic Optimization

Physics enhanced deep surrogates for nanoscale heat transfer

Up to 70% less training data than purely data-driven baselines, and about 5% error with only 300 high-fidelity simulations.

The problem

At the nanoscale, heat is carried by phonons and the usual diffusion law stops holding, so designing thermal materials means solving the phonon Boltzmann transport equation (BTE). Those solves are too slow to repeat inside a design loop. Fast macroscopic solvers can overestimate conductivity by hundreds of percent, and data-driven operator learners often need thousands of high-fidelity simulations.

The approach

The surrogate combines a differentiable Fourier solver with a neural generator, and couples them with uncertainty-driven active learning. The Fourier solver acts as a physical inductive bias, while the network learns geometry-dependent corrections and a mixing coefficient that interpolates between macroscopic and nanoscale behavior.

What we found

  • Training-data requirements drop by up to 70% compared with purely data-driven baselines.
  • Roughly 5% fractional error with only 300 high-fidelity BTE simulations.
  • Porous geometries spanning 12–85 W m−1 K−1 are designed with an average design error of 4%.
  • The learned mixing parameter recovers the ballistic–diffusive transition and improves out-of-distribution robustness.

Where it applies

Thermal management at small scales, such as microelectronics, thermoelectrics and energy conversion, and more generally any problem where a cheap, differentiable physics model can be corrected by learning.

Related publication: Physics Enhanced Deep Surrogates for the Phonon Boltzmann Transport Equation
